# Routing Matrix

Use this matrix first to choose a branch, then choose a product mode or brand system depth before choosing a visual trend. A route is a deliverable and behavior decision first; its visual language follows.

## Branch Gateway

| Branch | Choose when the primary deliverable is | Read next | Do not confuse with |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Product | A web/mobile app, SaaS, dashboard, devtool, commerce flow, game, content product, kiosk, or interactive workspace | Product modes below, then `visual-foundations.md` and `typography-content-brand.md` | A campaign site, identity presentation, or attractive static mockup |
| Brand / graphic | Strategy, naming, logo/identity, campaign, poster/social graphic, editorial/report, packaging, signage, template, infographic, or production artwork | `brand/routing-matrix.md`, `brand/visual-foundations.md`, then only the applicable brand/production references | UI art direction or a component library |
| Mixed | The assignment changes both identity/communication and an interactive product | Brand branch first for strategy/assets; product branch second for behavior/application | Blending both rule sets at once or averaging their reviews |

When unclear, identify what must be usable or deliverable at the end. Interaction states, task completion and browser behavior indicate the product branch. Recognition, message comprehension, cross-channel assets and production output indicate the brand branch. Use mixed only when both materially change; visual branding inside an otherwise stable product remains product work using approved assets.

For websites at the boundary, route by the primary success condition rather than the medium. A campaign, launch, event, or awareness site whose main job is recognition, message comprehension, and a simple CTA belongs to `brand/graphic`, with accessibility and browser checks applied to the implementation. A landing page whose main job requires form completion, account creation, purchase, configuration, data entry, persistent state, error handling, or recovery belongs to `product`, using approved brand assets. Use `mixed` only when the assignment also creates or materially changes the identity/campaign system and the interactive task flow.

## Branch Acceptance Examples

| Request | Expected route | Must not activate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Improve a bilingual SaaS settings flow using its approved logo | Product | Naming, logo creation, campaign, packaging or print production |
| Create a new identity, packaging family and launch posters | Brand/graphic: identity system plus required applications | Product IA, component states or browser-flow scoring unless an interactive artifact is also requested |
| Rebrand a service and redesign its customer app | Mixed: brand strategy/identity approval, then product mode and implementation | Simultaneous styling or one blended acceptance score |
| Audit an annual-report PDF and its editable template | Brand/graphic: editorial plus template/governance | Product review simply because the deliverable is viewed on screen |
| Apply a supplied campaign key visual to an existing landing-page form | Product with approved brand assets; use the brand branch only to verify supplied-asset rules when needed | Identity redesign or campaign expansion without user authorization |
| Create a short-lived event site whose only action is opening an approved registration link | Brand/graphic: campaign application, plus web accessibility checks | Product IA and state-system work that the deliverable does not need |
| Design a lead form with qualification logic, validation, saved progress and recovery | Product: brand/marketing/landing mode using approved assets | Campaign or identity creation unless separately requested |

The route passes only when the selected branch owns every requested deliverable and no inactive branch adds speculative work. If a product needs only an approved logo, load product asset rules, not the full identity branch. If a brand artifact has a small interactive preview, apply accessibility and state gates to that preview without converting the entire assignment into product design.

## Route Acceptance Gate

Do not start implementation until the route passes all applicable checks:

1. Name one primary branch and show which requested deliverable makes it responsible. `Mixed` must state the brand approval point before product translation.
2. List the minimum references to read now and the inactive capabilities that must not expand scope. Optional installed design skills or live sources cannot be required for the route to function.
3. Select at most one primary visual lineage and one counterweight that resolves a named tension. Treat research, standards, and validation sources as evidence, not extra styles.
4. Select the corresponding acceptance track: product gates, brand gates, or brand gates followed by product gates. Mixed work keeps findings and scores separate.
5. If two branches remain equally plausible because the final deliverable or success condition is unknown, ask one focused question. Otherwise choose the smallest defensible route, state the assumption, and continue.

A route fails when it loads unrelated branches, needs an unavailable helper without a local fallback, averages brand and product reviews, or cannot explain why its mode, lineage, and validation track fit the requested outcome.

## Candidate Modes

| Mode | Strong signals | Visual direction | Interaction priorities | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Operational SaaS / dashboard | Repeated work, comparison, status, tables, permissions | Restrained palette, high scan density, clear hierarchy, low decoration | Keyboard flow, filters, bulk actions, persistent context, empty/error/recovery states | Hero-sized type, card nesting, decorative gradients, hiding status in modals |
| Devtool / AI workspace | Panels, files, prompts, logs, technical users, frequent return visits | Precise neutral base, deliberate accent, pane structure, compact type | Shortcuts, focus management, resizable regions, streaming/loading feedback, undo | Treating a workbench like a marketing page, over-rounded controls, unbounded motion |
| Editorial / knowledge | Long reading, references, search, chronology, mixed media | Typography-led rhythm, readable measure, calm contrast, explicit section structure | TOC, anchors, search, progressive disclosure, reading position, link clarity | Dense dashboard chrome, oversized UI cards, decorative text effects |
| Brand / marketing / landing | First-visit persuasion, narrative, brand recognition, one main conversion | Brand signal in the first viewport, controlled art direction, strong media, clear CTA hierarchy | Scroll narrative, sticky sub-nav when useful, accessible CTA, responsive media crop | Generic split hero, brand skin copying, multiple competing CTAs, filler copy |
| Commerce / consumer | Browse, compare, trust, object recognition, purchase or save | Product/object is visible and inspectable, trust cues, purposeful emphasis | Search/filter/sort, gallery, sticky purchase/save, cart recovery, feedback | Atmospheric images that hide the object, dark patterns, unclear availability |
| Mobile / touch-first | Thumb use, short sessions, one-handed reach, constrained width | Hierarchy compressed without losing identity, strong contrast, stable controls | 44px targets, bottom-reachable actions, gestures with visible alternatives, keyboard avoidance | Desktop shrinkage, tiny icon-only controls, hover-only affordances |
| Creative / game / expressive | Direct manipulation, exploration, emotion, play, visual feedback | More expressive palette/assets/motion, but with a stable interaction grammar | Immediate feedback, gesture/drag behavior, pause/reset/undo, progress and recovery | Decoration that competes with the task, motion without state meaning, inaccessible controls |
| Data-heavy analysis | Metrics, trends, anomalies, repeated inspection | Quiet chrome, semantic color, strong alignment, density tuned to scan speed | Cross-filtering, drill-down, visible query state, loading/empty/no-data clarity | Rainbow charts, color-only meaning, decorative cards, hiding units/context |

## Scoring

Score each candidate 0-2 for: core job fit, usage context, content density, device fit, brand constraints, and implementation risk. Choose the highest total. A tie goes to the mode that preserves current user habits and requires fewer new interaction concepts. Do not average several modes into a visual collage.

## Style Axes To Make Explicit

Before choosing a reference, state the needed values on these axes:

- Energy: quiet, confident, warm, playful, dramatic.
- Density: airy, balanced, compact, information-dense.
- Contrast: soft, balanced, high-contrast.
- Edge language: sharp, restrained radius, friendly radius, expressive shape.
- Type system: locale/script coverage, UI family, optional display family, numeric/code family, role scale, line height, and text-color hierarchy.
- Content voice: direct, technical, reassuring, editorial, playful, or high-consequence precise.
- Color behavior: neutral base with one accent, semantic multi-color, monochrome, image-led, or high-energy controlled palette.
- Image role: object proof, product UI, editorial content, brand atmosphere, or none.
- Motion role: none, feedback only, transition explanation, or expressive interaction.
- Brand asset role: approved logo, generated identity, provisional wordmark, product imagery, or none.

These are design decisions, not keywords to paste into a generator. Each selected value must trace to the product job or user context.

## Typography And Content Fit

- Operational, service, finance, health, and data modes favor full-CJK sans UI systems, restrained headings, explicit wording, readable secondary text, and aligned numerals.
- Editorial modes may pair sans controls with a CJK serif reading/display family; the reading measure and rhythm matter more than decorative contrast.
- Brand and marketing modes may use an expressive display family for short headlines or a wordmark, but body, forms, navigation, and legal copy remain highly readable.
- Devtools use monospace for code, logs, and identifiers only; Chinese UI, prose, and navigation remain in a full-CJK interface family.
- Commerce typography must make product name, price, unit, availability, variants, delivery, and purchase consequences easy to compare.
- Child-facing products use larger, distinct glyphs and simple words; high-consequence products avoid thin type, vague labels, jokes, and experimental reading patterns.

For detailed role sizes, mixed Chinese-English behavior, content patterns, and logo dispatch, read [typography-content-brand.md](typography-content-brand.md).

## Interaction Selection Heuristics

- Repeated actions favor persistent navigation, visible filters, keyboard shortcuts, and stable dimensions.
- Exploration favors progressive disclosure, previews, breadcrumbs/TOC, and reversible choices.
- High-consequence actions favor confirmation at the point of risk, clear status, undo, and recovery.
- One primary action favors a single visual accent and a predictable location; do not color every action as primary.
- Mobile variants should preserve the task order while changing reachability, grouping, and input method.
- When introducing an unfamiliar icon or gesture, pair it with an accessible label or visible alternative.
